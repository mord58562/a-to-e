============================
1. DIFFICULTY (binding - overhaul v3, 2026-06-01)
============================

Five tiers, 1-5/5. Difficulty is the COMPLEXITY OF REASONING required
to land on the correct answer - not stem length, not topic rarity,
not vocabulary.

  1/5  Single-fact recall or single-step application. One data point
       discriminates. 10-20 sec. Reserve for fundamentals that every
       Y4 student should default-answer. <=5% of any batch.
  2/5  Integrate 2-3 data points and choose between PLAUSIBLE
       distractors. Lead-in is "most appropriate next step", not
       "what is it". 30-60 sec of real reasoning. ~50% baseline.
  3/5  Multi-step pathway with trade-offs, OR application of an
       AU-specific cut-off / threshold / dose-rule, with competing
       considerations. 60-90 sec.
  4/5  Atypical or contested presentation, anti-pattern recognition,
       OR niche sub-specialty fact PLUS multi-step reasoning. Niche
       fact alone is never L4. Target 15-20% of bank. 90-150 sec.
  5/5  L4 plus one or more of: a calculation under uncertainty, a
       disconfirming finding that kills the textbook default and
       forces reasoning past it, competing guidelines requiring you
       to choose one cut-off, a multi-branch algorithm where the
       wrong branch is genuinely tempting. ~5% target. Reads as a
       "stop-and-think" item, not "I recognise this." Niche topic
       alone is NEVER L5.

For a 30-Q batch aim ~1 L1 / ~12 L2 / ~10 L3 / ~5 L4 / ~2 L5.
For a 50-Q batch aim ~2 L1 / ~22 L2 / ~17 L3 / ~7 L4 / ~3 L5.

>=50% of L4-L5 must centre on COMMON presentations (sore throat, PPH,
ACS, paracetamol overdose, postnatal depression, asthma exacerbation)
where reasoning depth EARNS the rating - not rare-topic recognition.

History (do not regress): the bank shifted down by one level on
2026-06-01 (overhaul v3). What was 5/5 is now 4/5, 4/5 is now 3/5,
3/5 is now 2/5, 2/5 is now 1/5. New 5/5 is a separate even-harder
tier (see above). Every shifted question has `difficulty_prior_v3`
recording its pre-shift value; use this only for back-traceability,
never as the displayed difficulty.

Hard demote-to-discard patterns (= telegraphed answer):
- Stem names the gold-standard treatment or pathognomonic triad.
- Stem pre-states the diagnosis with a lead-in that just asks "next?".
- Patient/clinician preference pre-commits the choice.
- Risk-escalation telegraphing ("compounding the risk", "further
  increasing the risk").
- Stem's last sentence describes the management plan the correct
  option will use (e.g. "consented antenatally to internal manoeuvres
  and breech extraction for a non-cephalic second twin" - that
  sentence telegraphs internal podalic version as the answer).
- "Offer X, Y, or Z and support patient choice" as the correct option
  when no clinical feature actually discriminates - replace lead-in
  with "what would you FIRST recommend given..." and let the rationale
  acknowledge the shared decision.

2. VOICE
============================

Write every field as one Australian registrar writing teaching notes
for a final-year student on the team: the register of an eTG entry or
an RCH CPG, plain and exact. Not a textbook chapter, not an essay.

- Use the abbreviations the student meets on the ward and in the
  notes (ADHD, PTSD, HbA1c, PaCO2, LMWH, CTG, PO). Expand one on its
  first use in the question unless it is universal on an Australian
  ward (ECG, IV, CT, FBC, GP); never spell out a universal one.
- Quantities are digits with units: "a 44-year-old woman", "for 4
  weeks", "0.5 mg/kg", "in 6 weeks' time". Hyphenate a compound
  modifier before its noun: "first-line therapy", "long-acting
  insulin", "non-tender abdomen", "intra-articular injection".
- Parentheses, semicolons and colons are ordinary punctuation. Use the
  mark the sentence needs. A sentence bent to avoid one is the wrong
  sentence.
- Sentence length follows the content. A threshold can be six words;
  a mechanism can take thirty. Split a sentence that chains three
  clauses on "and", "because" or "so".
- State the clinical fact: the drug, finding, threshold or guideline
  difference. Never describe how the question was built.
- The only banned characters and words are the ones
  scripts/check_tokens.py lists (section 7). Nothing else is off
  limits for looking generated.

============================
3. STEM CRAFT
============================

Write the stem exactly as long as the clinical scenario requires - no
length floors, no padding. Typical exam-range stems sit at 110-180
words; trim ruthlessly to that band unless the case genuinely needs
more (multi-step screening pathway, complex CTG cycle, sequential
results). Use PARAGRAPH BREAKS when a stem exceeds ~80 words so it
reads as scannable clinical prose, not a wall of text.

LATE-ANCHOR PRINCIPLE: bury exactly ONE discriminating finding in the
second-to-last sentence (or final clause before the lead-in). The rest
of the stem must be plausibly consistent with three or more options.
The signature finding is the only thing that flips the question.

LAY-LANGUAGE PRINCIPLE: when the PATIENT or family reports a finding,
use lay language ("dark coloured urine", "long thin lump in his right
upper tummy", "starts crying suddenly, draws his legs up, then
settles"). Reserve medical English for CLINICIAN-observed findings
(auscultation, imaging interpretation, exam descriptions). Telegraph
phrases like "currant-jelly stool", "sausage-shaped mass", "colicky
abdominal pain" are stem-level auto-rejects unless the lead-in is
"most likely diagnosis".

DISCONFIRMING-FINDING PRINCIPLE (L3-L5): every L3+ question should
contain at least one finding that rules out the textbook default
differential, forcing the candidate to reason past it ("DAT negative,
blood groups matched" kills HDN; "previous Hb >110 three years ago"
kills inherited thalassaemia despite Mediterranean heritage; "renal
function and electrolytes within normal limits" kills uraemia/SIADH).

NUMERIC NOT ADJECTIVAL: "BP 165/110", not "severely hypertensive".
"Myometrium 2 mm between gestational sac and bladder", not "thin
myometrium". The candidate is expected to know the threshold.

Typical AU-MD vignette shape when needed: demographics + presenting
complaint + relevant history + exam + investigations + medications.
Use the `data_table` field for structured findings (vitals, labs,
imaging, drugs); do NOT also state them in the stem prose.

The candidate IS the treating doctor. Never frame the stem from a
nurse / pharmacist / midwife / student observer perspective. Other
professionals appearing in the scene is fine - the question is always
"what do you, the doctor, do next".

OPENING: open with the patient ("A 34-year-old woman..."). Name your
role or setting only when it changes the answer: what can be done on
site, who holds the decision, a confidentiality or referral call ("in
a remote clinic without CT", "as her GP"). You are the treating doctor
whether or not the stem says so. Vary ages and settings the way a real
clinic list does; they are data, not decoration.

Hard anti-patterns in stems (auto-reject):
- Author meta-commentary ("(this hooks the X distractor)",
  "rules out [A-E]", "red herring)", "tests the X anti-pattern").
- Risk-escalation telegraphing (above).
- Doubled structured data (data_table contradicts or repeats prose).
- Markdown asterisks `**bold**` anywhere in the stem or lead-in.
- Leaked back-of-house keys ("Differential ruled out by echo").
- Lead-in guideline-name leak ("best matches RCH and ISPAD guidance").
  Use neutral lead-ins ("most appropriate next step", "single best
  initial intervention").
- Pertinent-negative pile-up (chains of "no X, no Y, no Z, no W"
  beyond 2 items - they telegraph the answer). Max 2 explicit closures
  per stem ("There is no recent overseas travel.", "She has no
  personal or family history of venous thromboembolism."). 3+ becomes
  a pile-up auto-reject.
- Stem-level diagnosis disclosure followed by a withholding lead-in
  ("...this likely represents pre-eclampsia..." then "Which one of
  the following is the most likely diagnosis?") - choose ONE mode.
- Garbled text: run-on words (no space between sentence end and next),
  mid-word truncations, missing punctuation, sentences ending
  mid-clause. Codepoint-grep before commit.

VITALS FORMAT: one comma-separated prose sentence, fixed order: pulse
rate / blood pressure / respiratory rate / SpO2 / temperature
(maternal first, then fetal HR for obstetric). Either give all five
or compress to "vital signs within normal limits". Do NOT mix.

DATA TABLE: keys must be flat strings, NOT nested objects. The
renderer flattens nested values defensively but the schema expects
flat. Either give one key per finding ("Cervical length", "Funnelling",
"Fetal presentation") or compress to "Ultrasound findings".

REFERENCE RANGES: every load-bearing lab gets its range in parentheses
on the same line: "haemoglobin 90 g/L (120-160)". Naked numbers without
ranges signal to the candidate that the lab is decorative, which is
itself a leak if the lab IS load-bearing. Omit decorative labs entirely.
EXCEPTION: a normal lab line whose normality is itself decision-relevant
(rule-out) may be written as "within normal limits" instead of the full
value+range. AU RCPA reference ranges; for haematology pick MCH (pg) or
MCHC (g/L), never mix.

DISCONFIRMING-FINDING POSITION: late stem (second-to-last sentence) OR
sentence 2 are both legitimate anchor positions. Distribute across a
batch; do not always-place late.

BEDSIDE-TEST DATA: name the test rather than the abstracted finding.
"Weber lateralises to the right", "Romberg positive", "Cover test
reveals an outward shift of the left eye" - not "there is unilateral
hearing loss on the right". Forces interpretation, not recognition.

============================
4. OPTIONS (binding parity rules)
============================

Five options A-E, exactly one with `correct: true`.

ANSWER-LENGTH PARITY (hard): max:min character ratio across the 5
option `text` fields <= 1.35. NEVER write a correct option that is
markedly longer, more specific, more qualified, or uniquely contains
a key clinical word (a dose, "with airway clearance", "no
contraindication") that no distractor has. Either pad the distractors
with comparable plausible specificity OR shorten the correct option.

DOSE-FORMAT PARITY: all options use the same units, the same separator
style, the same route abbreviation. Inconsistency telegraphs.

NO DIAGNOSIS-LEAK: when the stem withholds the diagnosis to test
diagnostic + management reasoning, NO option may name that diagnosis.
Options describe ACTIONS; the rationale carries the diagnosis.
Exception: lead-ins of the form "what is the most likely diagnosis?".

NO PERTINENT-NEGATIVE PARENTHETICALS in the correct option text. A
trailing "(no hypertension contraindication)" is a leak.

NO "FOLLOW LOCAL GUIDELINES" non-options. Any option that reduces to
"do what the local guideline says" / "as per institutional policy"
trivializes by elimination. Replace with a substantive distractor.

Correct-letter distribution across the batch: roughly even (avoid
all-A; ~3-4 per letter for 15-Q, ~6 per letter for 30-Q).

PARALLEL-GRAMMAR BREAK: parallel grammar across all 5 options is the
default. A deliberate break IS allowed when one option lives on a
different decision axis (medical vs surgical, screen vs diagnose,
observe vs intervene), but its rationale must contain one sentence
saying in clinical terms why that kind of choice is not the one this
patient needs yet. Accidental breaks (cosmetic, draft artefact) get
fixed during self-audit.

CLOSE ALTERNATIVES: when a distractor sits right beside the correct
option (ligamentum arteriosum vs ductus arteriosus, MCH vs MCHC,
cefotaxime vs ceftriaxone, salpingostomy vs salpingectomy), its
rationale MUST state the clinical difference between the two, not
just "X is incorrect".

============================
5. RATIONALES + EXPLANATIONS
============================

Each option carries a `rationale` of 1-3 sentences. Every rationale
ends with an inline source citation by name (e.g. "(Source: RCH CPG:
Bronchiolitis)" or "(eTG; AMH)").

The correct option's rationale says why it is right for THIS patient.

A distractor rationale says why the option is wrong for this patient,
then gives one fact the student can use elsewhere, without restating
the option in other words. That fact is one of: the situation in which
this option would be the right choice (what would have to be
different in the stem), the Australian guideline difference, or the
specific error the option represents. Write it as an ordinary sentence
whose subject is the drug, test or plan, not a stamped fragment such
as "Would be correct if...". Do not close with a verdict sentence
("Would never be correct", "Would be wrong"); the screen already marks
the option wrong.

Words in this prompt are for you, not the student. Never print them in
any field: near pair, near-miss, anti-pattern, discriminator, hook,
telegraph, late anchor, disconfirming finding, decision axis,
carve-out, red herring, distractor, candidate, trap. Write the
clinical relationship each one stands for.

Each option has a `source_refs` array of strings that match labels
in the question's top-level `sources` array.

`explanation.summary` is 3-5 sentences of broader clinical context
the question lives in (the wider pathway, indications,
contraindications, what happens if missed). It is NOT a restatement
of the correct rationale, and it ends by giving the student their
bearings on the underlying condition.

`explanation.key_points` is an array of 3-5 plain statements of fact,
each able to stand alone as a line in the student's notes. No
"Label:" prefixes ("Treatment: ...", "Rationale: ..."). Use "X, not
Y" only when the wrong belief is what the question tests.

`explanation.pearls` is ONE specific, memorable clinical fact that
the rationales and summary do not already contain, in one or two
sentences and usually under 30 words: a named drug, threshold,
timeframe or sign. NOT formulaic ("Remember to consider...", "Always
check...", "Key learning point...", "Don't forget..."). Build it from
the fact itself: no maxim, no setup then a dash then a punchline, no
"because ..., so ..." chain restating the mechanism above.

============================
6. SOURCES + TAGS
============================

`sources` is a 2+ entry array of {label, url?} objects. Australian
first (RCH CPG, RANZCOG, KEMH, Therapeutic Guidelines / eTG, AMH,
ASHM, COPE, NHFA/CSANZ, RACGP, NHMRC, ANZICS, ARA). Then international
backups (NICE, BMJ Best Practice, UpToDate, StatPearls, Cochrane).

`tags` is an array of MAX 3 short tags from the live vocabulary.
Guideline names go in `sources`, not `tags`. Generic modifiers
(severe, common, complex, first-line, acute, chronic) are NOT tags.
Drug names, condition names, and well-known acronyms (DKA, PPH, ACS,
SSRI) are good tags.

7. UNITS + SPELLING
============================

AU SI units throughout: mmol/L, micromol/L, g/L, x10^9/L, mmHg, mcg
(NOT μg), kg, °C.

AU spellings: paediatric, gynaecology, haematology, haemorrhage,
fetal, oesophagus, oedema, anaemia, leukaemia, orthopaedic, fibre,
colour, behaviour, signalling, caesarean, dyspnoea, diarrhoea, faeces.
Exception: drug INN "magnesium sulfate" follows AMH.

Banned tokens (ZERO tolerated). scripts/check_tokens.py enforces all
of these and is the list of record:
- Em-dashes (U+2014). Rewrite the sentence with the mark it needs: a
  full stop, comma, colon or parentheses. A spaced hyphen dropped into
  the same slot is the same habit.
- Any initialism standing in for "Aboriginal and Torres Strait
  Islander". Spell the phrase out in full, or name only the applicable
  half when writing about a specific person.
- The word the checker flags as C1. Use "primary", "core", "main",
  "live" or "promoted".
- Markdown emphasis markers.

8. AU units + AU spellings throughout.
9. No author meta-commentary, no risk-escalation telegraph, no
   doubled data_table, no lead-in guideline-name leak, no
   "follow local guidelines" option, no diagnosis-leak in options.
10. Read one rationale, the summary and the pearl aloud as the
